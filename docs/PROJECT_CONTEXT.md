# 2D Doll Project Context

This document preserves durable design intent, terminology, constraints, and the creative north star. It is not a feature-status page or historical log. Use the [README](../README.md) for the concise entry point, [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) for operating rules, and [pass reports](pass-reports/README.md) for evidence from individual development passes.

## Core Purpose

2D Doll is a modular scene-construction system for creating expressive illustrated characters, poses, interactions, and finished comic or panel compositions from reusable parts.

The goal is not to accumulate one-off assets or hard-coded scenes. A useful primitive should unlock many outcomes and remain compatible with other primitives.

## Creative North Star

> The goal is not a library of predetermined pictures.

> It is a 2D illustrated performance system where a limited set of characters, parts, poses, expressions, interactions, clothing, props, environments, and presentation tools can generate a very large number of readable scenes.

The recurring design question is:

> What new combinations become possible because this exists?

## Combinatorial / PSG Principle

The system should be understood through:

**STATE → CONSTRAINTS → POSSIBILITIES → PRESENTATION**

This sequence is a design lens rather than a mandated implementation architecture.

- **State** describes the editable facts of a scene: characters, parts, clothing, expression, pose, interaction, props, effects, background, dialogue, camera, and composition.
- **Constraints** preserve compatibility and meaning: anatomy, attachment, joint ranges, parent-child behavior, interaction relationships, layering, and other invariants.
- **Possibilities** are the many coherent outcomes generated by recombining state within constraints.
- **Presentation** frames those outcomes as readable panels through placement, depth, effects, dialogue, camera, and export.

Characters, body parts, clothing, expressions, poses, interactions, props, effects, backgrounds, dialogue, camera, and composition should remain separable where practical. Complex scenes should emerge from compatible simpler systems.

## Character Creator

Character Creator is the responsibility that defines reusable character identity and construction, including:

- proportions and body-part artwork,
- faces and expressions,
- hair, clothing, and accessories,
- attachment points,
- character-specific constraints.

Its output should carry enough structure and semantics to be posed and presented without collapsing into a single flattened asset.

## Poser

Poser is the responsibility that defines character state and relationships, including:

- joint articulation and whole-body movement,
- facing and orientation,
- expression and clothing state,
- one- or two-character posing,
- props and interaction points,
- reusable poses and interactions.

Posing should preserve meaningful internal and cross-object relationships instead of encoding only final screen coordinates.

## Placer

Placer is the responsibility that turns posed characters into finished panels, including:

- background and foreground,
- position, scale, layering, and depth,
- framing and camera,
- effects, lighting, or tint where appropriate,
- dialogue, thought bubbles, and captions,
- final panel export.

Character Creator, Poser, and Placer are conceptual responsibilities, not necessarily three applications. Their boundaries must remain interoperable.

## Articulation Model

A character is ultimately a structured articulated object, not merely a draggable flattened image. Its model may contain joints, body segments, anchors, constraints, render layers, and semantic interaction points.

Articulation should remain anatomically understandable even when deliberately exaggerated. Future controls should support visible or selectable pivots, sensible rotation ranges, translation where appropriate, whole-character movement, clear selection, and predictable parent-child movement.

The precise representation is still **SPECULATIVE** until an architecture pass evaluates and records it.

## Mechanical Rig Truth vs Artwork

Mechanical rig truth and final artwork are separate concerns.

- **Mechanical rig truth** describes structure, joints, hierarchy, anchors, constraints, and state behavior.
- **Final artwork** describes the approved visual appearance rendered through that structure.

Placeholder artwork, limb proportions, masks, sprites, and temporary assets are not approved visual design. Rig architecture should survive later artwork replacement whenever practical. Visual polish must not obscure incorrect rig behavior.

## Interaction Points

Interaction points are a major combinatorial primitive. Semantic anchors may eventually include:

- `hold`, `grab`, `hand`, `wrist`, `shoulder`, `waist`, and `face`,
- `look-at`, `carry`, `embrace`, and `lean-on`,
- `sit-on`, `stand-on`, `prop-grip`, `contact`, and `target`.

An interaction should describe relationships between objects rather than permanently bake one pose. Where useful, a relationship may include a source anchor, target anchor, orientation, offset, constraints, and optional pose assistance.

The vocabulary and data model remain **DESIGNED** at a high level but are not implemented.

## Reusable Poses and Interactions

Reusable poses should express meaningful character state in a way that can adapt across compatible characters. Reusable interactions should coordinate two or more compatible objects through semantic relationships.

Intended examples include holding hands, carrying, hugging, grabbing, sitting together, fighting, and exchanging objects. These examples are requirements context, not an implemented feature list.

Reuse must not require every participant to have identical proportions or artwork. Future architecture should define compatibility, adaptation, assistance, and graceful fallback behavior.

## Non-Destructive State

Editing should be reversible and non-destructive where practical. Prefer parameters over duplicated variants, references over destructive flattening, and explicit state over changes hidden in artwork.

Derived presentation should not silently destroy the character, pose, or relationship data from which it came. Exported final imagery may be flattened, but editable project state should retain its meaningful structure.

## Interoperability

The three responsibilities must exchange structured state without losing meaning. In particular:

- Character Creator output must remain usable by Poser.
- A pose created in Poser should transfer to Placer without losing internal relationships.
- Placer presentation changes should not corrupt reusable character or pose definitions.
- Shared primitives need explicit compatibility and versioning behavior when implementation begins.

Interoperability is an architectural constraint, not evidence that a file format or API currently exists.

## Design Rules

- Prefer reusable primitives over one-off outcomes.
- Prefer parameters over duplicated variants.
- Prefer relationships over absolute coordinates.
- Prefer semantic anchors over arbitrary transforms.
- Prefer reversible, non-destructive editing.
- Prefer data-driven systems over special cases.
- Prefer graceful fallbacks over fragile perfection.
- Keep mechanics inspectable and behavior predictable.
- Preserve separability among identity, pose, interaction, environment, and presentation where practical.
- Evaluate both newly enabled combinations and possibilities an implementation could restrict.

## Development Discipline

Functional truth comes before polish: selection, manipulation, predictability, composition with other systems, and state preservation must work before appearance is refined.

Repair root causes where practical. Do not imply completeness. Keep current documentation aligned with source reality, while preserving historical reports as evidence of what was true at the time.

Every development pass must create a permanent report under `docs/pass-reports/` following the protocol in its [ledger](pass-reports/README.md).

## Reality States

Use the following terms precisely:

- **SPECULATIVE:** Idea only.
- **DESIGNED:** Behavior or architecture has been defined but does not necessarily exist in source.
- **IMPLEMENTED:** Exists in source.
- **TESTED:** Verified systematically.
- **VALIDATED:** Demonstrated useful in the intended creative workflow.

Automated tests alone do not establish creative-workflow validation. Desired behavior written here remains DESIGNED until source and evidence support a stronger state.

## Questions Future Architecture Must Preserve

Future architecture decisions should answer without prematurely narrowing the solution:

- What are the smallest stable primitives for character identity, articulation, pose, interaction, and presentation?
- How is semantic state represented independently from rendering technology?
- How do characters with different proportions share poses and interactions?
- How are anchors, constraints, hierarchy, and render layers expressed and versioned?
- How does a reusable interaction adapt, assist, or fall back when an expected anchor is unavailable?
- How does Poser transfer state to Placer without losing internal or cross-character relationships?
- Which edits are reversible, and how is editable state separated from flattened export?
- How can artwork be replaced without invalidating mechanical rig truth?
- How are compatibility and constraint failures explained to the user?
- What new combinations does each primitive enable, and what possibilities might it accidentally restrict?
- What is the smallest experiment that can test the architecture before broad feature implementation?
